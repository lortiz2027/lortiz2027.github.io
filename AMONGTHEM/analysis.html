PROJECT ANALYSIS & SYNTHESIS

Summary

1/4

On August 30, 2026, the group gathered four volunteer players—Bothsurge (Nicolas Joaquin Bermido), Rgll0 (Ruzzel Angelo Laugo), quirsten (Gian Quirsten Ramirez), and Epicname (Raina Rayelle Bautista)—to observe the communication situation occurring in the interaction of players within the game “Among Us”. Each player was asked for free, prior, and informed consent to participate in this study. The observation setup was conducted online via Google Meet, and all pertinent data were collected through online observation and video recording. After the players were briefed on the setup, the players were separated into different meetings, with at least one group member observing them. During the duration of the game, the players were asked to share their screen for observation and data collection. In addition, the meetings were recorded for further analysis.

Summary

2/4

Upon entering the game, the players are given a role: imposter or crewmate, with each game having one imposter and three crewmates. The crewmates complete tasks around the ship, while the imposter kills and sabotages the crewmates whilst attempting to blend in. Both the crewmates and imposter act as senders and receivers in this communication setup. The messages could either be information given by crewmates pointing to a potential impostor, a crewmate vouching for their or a fellow crewmate’s innocence, and/or false accusations spread by the imposter against an innocent crewmate. The players could only verbally communicate with each other using the in-game chat box during emergency meetings. After the discussions between players, each player is given the choice to vote for which player they think is the imposter or skip the vote. If the majority of players vote for one player, that player will be ejected. If the number of votes is tied or a majority voted to skip, then no one will be ejected. The effect of the emergency meetings could be players drawing other players’ suspicions, the imposter changing playstyles to strengthen alibis, or the game simply ending due to a failed or successful eject. Barriers in this situation would be internet interruptions, server-side issues, or problems with the device, which was observed in the second game with Bothsurge getting disconnected from the game because of his internet connection.

Summary

3/4

In the first game, quirsten (with the role of imposter) manages to deceive Bothsurge, Rgll0, and Epicname (all crewmates) by faking tasks and directing other crewmates’ suspicions onto Bothsurge. quirsten won the game via a kill on Rgll0, and persuading Epicname to vote Bothsurge out of the ship.

After a small break, the second game took place in which Epicname was given the imposter role. Rgll0 was mostly focused on doing their tasks throughout the ship. Bothsurge experienced a communication barrier and disconnected from the game, which left Epicname needing only one kill to win the game. Epicname eventually eliminated quirsten, winning the second game.

Summary

4/4

Observations:

Sender and Receiver: All players acted as both sender and receiver throughout the emergency meeting.

Message: During meetings, the crewmates shared information relevant to deducing the imposter among the players. The imposter attempted to give false information to deceive the crewmates and front as one of them.

Channel: The players talked to each other using a designated chatbox featured within the game.

Feedback: The crewmates opted to skip the meeting.

Effect: The crewmates’ suspicions are directed to one of the other crewmates.

Noise/Barriers: Throughout the observation, it was seen that there were moments where players disconnected due to poor internet connection, disrupting their gameplay.

VERBAL COMMUNICATION CUES

Pragmatics


In the communication situation, pragmatics serves a role in determining meaning based on context. In both emergency meetings in the first observation, the word “white” serves as a deictic reference pointing directly to player Bothsurge’s avatar rather than a literal color shade (Hulatt, 2023). Within the chat, utterances also perform highly specific functions. For instance, the phrase “yo white been awfully close bro” by player Rgll0 acts as an indirect speech act. On the surface, it states physical proximity, but pragmatically, it functions as an accusation towards player Bothsurge being impostor. The players also violate Grice’s Maxim of Quantity by repeatedly asking “why”, of which omission forces the accuser, player Rgll0, to provide more evidence for his claim (Cipro, 2024). When player Bothsurge replies with “bro”, it serves as an expressive speech act, replacing a much longer sentence of a defensive exclamation of innocence. The phrase “tracking me” also relies on a shared understanding of game mechanics. While it usually implies GPS in normal conversations, in this context, it signifies an impostor following a player with the intention of eliminating them. In the second meeting, the exclamation “BRUH” from player quirsten functions similarly as an exasperated discourse marker of absolute disbelief, asking how anyone could possibly suspect them. When player Bothsurge responds with laughter “HAHAHAHA”, it does not communicate happiness, but rather pragmatically acts as a conversational deflection to reject player quirsten’s claim entirely.

Semantics


From a semantic or literal perspective, the dialogue exhibits semantic bleaching (Merriam-Webster Dictionary, n.d.). The literal definition of the word “bro” exclaimed by player Bothsurge that literally denotes a brother or peer is completely “bleached” out and functions purely as an emotional marker.

Syntax


In terms of syntax, distinct syntactic features of African American Vernacular English (AAVE) are highly visible in the gameplay as modern digital slang (International Center for Language Studies, 2024). The phrase “white be tracking” features the AAVE grammatical rule known as the habitual aspect or invariant “be.” In standard English, stating “White is tracking me” only implies an action is happening at this exact moment. However, the usage of the invariant “be” denotes a continuous and repetitive behavior over time, meaning white or player Bothsurge has been continuously stalking player Rgll0 throughout the game .

In addition, the phrase “yo white[‘s] been awfully close bro” demonstrates zero copula or auxiliary deletion (often referred to as a copula drop). In AAVE, linking verbs (is/are) or auxiliary helping verbs (has/have) can be completely omitted if standard English conventions would normally allow them to be contracted (e.g., changing “White has been” to “White’s been”, and then dropping the sound entirely to become “White been”) (International Center for Language Studies, 2024). Such omission is not a grammatical error, but rather follows AAVE that allows for rapid communication. In the second meeting, players quirsten and Bothsurge both also use single-word sentences like “WHITE” and “gian”, in which they deliberately skip any standard English parts of speech to maximize typing speed against the game timer.

Finally, typing in all-caps, most demonstrated in the second emergency meeting, serves as a visual prosody to represent shouting (Academy of American Poets, 2023). Capitalized words like “WHITE”, “BRUH”, and “HAHAHAHA” are indicative of high vocal volume and emotional stress.


NONVERBAL COMMUNICATION CUES

Chronemics


The communicative use of time, or chronemics, dictates the pacing and stress levels of the gameplay, which is most especially felt during the in-game meetings. The countdown timer of these emergency meetings non-verbally creates a high-pressure environment to the players via extrapersonal communication between the game system and the players, signalling to them that they must reach a consensus before the timer runs out.

Proxemics


Proxemics, or spatial behavior, serves as a primary source of paranoia during the gameplay. Throughout the first round, player Bothsurge follows player Rgll0 through a long rotation spanning from Admin to the Cafeteria, Weapons, Shields, and Storage. While player Bothsurge likely intended such tracking as a non-verbal sign of vouching or alliance, Rgll0 interpreted this proximity as stalking and a direct threat, ultimately acting as a trigger for the first emergency meeting. Later in the round, the threat of proximity turns fatal when player quirsten kills player Rgll0 inside Electrical in front of player Bothsurge. Because they were in an isolated room, quirsten was able to trap Bothsurge spatially and was able to frame him during the subsequent report of the dead body.

Kinesics


From a kinesic standpoint, player movement is heavily weaponized for deception throughout the gameplay. As impostors, quirsten and Epicname utilize such deceptive kinesic techniques to fake tasks by standing perfectly still over task stations in order to replicate the behavior of a productive crewmate and mask their true identity from onlookers.

Optics


In terms of optics, environmental visual cues are used to signal presence and awareness across the ship. For instance, player quirsten goes to Security to check the cameras in both observations, which signals to any passing player underneath the security cameras that an observing eye is present via the blinking red camera. Such a mechanic transforms the camera box into a non-verbal deterrent and subtly alters how players navigate the corridors based on the visible light cue.


COMMUNICATION BARRIERS

Time in Communication

1/4

For a game where communication is limited to the abrupt non-verbal player interactions and emergency meetings—time constitutes a significant barrier within Among Us.

Meetings are a time-limited event within Among Us that either occurs when a player reports a body or starts an emergency meeting. These meetings are governed by two distinct periods: discussion and voting, both of which are limited through time. Such limitations create temporal constraints in how players communicate—a fundamental barrier that is then exacerbated by lack of paralinguistic and non-verbal cues that originates from the games characteristic text-based communication. A player must recall the events of the game leading up to the meeting, communicate the relevant information, encode multiple messages at the same time, and determine if the aggregate knowledge of the group justifies a sufficient reason for a vote.

Time in Communication

2/4

Beyond game timers, unstable internet connection acts as a direct technical barrier that further disrupts the temporal flow of communication. High latency, lag, or sudden network disconnections interrupt message transmission and completely remove players from active discussions—as observed during the second game when Bothsurge was disconnected due to connection issues. This physical noise deprives the group of crucial testimony and forces remaining players to make decisions without complete information.

Time in Communication

3/4

During Rgll0’s emergency meeting in the first round, he accused Bothsurge of “being awfully close” which was met with an immediate response from Bothsurge replying with “bro”. That “bro”, which simultaneously functions as a pragmatic “no” and a pseudo-epistimological response to an accusation, reveals an underlying characteristic of time within the game, that is efficiency in the face of temporal pressure. It demonstrates how players must rapidly produce and interpret claims before the voting period ends—leaving no room for extended cross-examination and testimonial validation.

Time in Communication

4/4

Further into the game, at the second meeting of the first round, time played another relevant function in the form of typing speed and ping—the time it takes to write an information and the time it takes for that data (message) to travel to the server and back respectively. The meeting was caused by Bothsurge reporting Rgll0’s dead body. In Bothsurge, Rgll0, and quirsten’s recordings, it should be noted that quirsten was responsible for Rgll0’s death. Bothsurge was present in electrical during quirsten’s killing of Rgll0 and should therefore have been able to testify for quirsten’s identity as the imposter. However, Epicname was in storage after finishing the garbage task which left her in a crucial and uncomfortable position to decide the true identity of the imposter based solely on the testimony of Bothsurge and quirsten. By virtue of testimonial speed, Epicname made a decision that is grounded solely on the fact that quirsten was the first to make an accusation. Essentially, a judgement in consideration of time and therefore highlights the importance of time in communication.

Prejudice and Credibility in Group Functionality

1/2

A significant, if not an inherent characteristic within, derivatives of the mafia game that involves a split second analysis of each participant presents a fundamental hindrance towards the group's overall functionality—that is one's own prejudice.

Prejudice becomes consequential when participants rely on heuristics to determine the credibility of each other's testimony. Players' statements are held at different epistemological weights based not only on the content but rather in consideration groups' perception—often micro-perception—of that player's previous actions.

Prejudice and Credibility in Group Functionality

2/2

In Among Us, non-verbal behaviour becomes the foundational lens in which a player is judged and given credibility—given the lack of in-game verbal communication. Actions, such as following, abrupt and arbitrary pauses, circling, and other movements under a proximic context can and will be interpreted as a potential malicious act regardless of intent. This can be observed during the first game where-in Bothsurge’s unusual proximity with respect to Rgll0 led Rgll0 to believe that Bothsurge’s actions harbored a malicious intent. Bothsurge’s repeated proximity does not conceal an inherent threat towards Rgll0; the behaviour was as likely to be an attempt to establish an alibi and at the same time monitor another individual. The same observable conduct can therefore receive diametrically opposing epistemological interpretations which can later result in the incorrect identification of the culpable individual—which is precisely what occurred when Bothsurge was voted out.

Recommendations

1/3

First,   fix the connection problem by playing on a single local Wi-Fi or LAN Network. A LAN network only covers a small distance but can transmit data quickly between connected devices, usually faster than standard internet connection (SOPHOS, n.d.) . This stops players from getting disconnected like Bothsurge did in the second game and keeps the game fair.

Recommendations

2/3

Second, using a third-party app like Discord for voice chat during meetings makes talking much easier. Speaking out loud lets players explain themselves faster, avoids typing delays, and reintroduces tone of voice so players can tell if someone is lying rather than just guessing based on how close their avatars were walking.

Recommendations

3/3

Lastly, adjust the game settings to give players more time to talk to each other. Increasing the time of discussion before voting prevents players from rushing to vote out the first person accused in the chat. Players should have the time needed to state where they were, what they saw, and what task they did during the time of report instead of relying on short confusing messages. This gives the crewmates the ability to figure out the facts based on cumulative information.
