PROJECT INTRODUCTION

PROJECT DESCRIPTION


Among Us is an online multiplayer game where players act as astronauts in a spaceship, while (a) hidden impostor/s secretly sabotage(s) the ship. The players are divided into crewmates and (an) impostor/s. The crewmates work together to complete tasks within the ship and identify the hidden impostor/s. If they are able to complete the tasks and/or are able to identify and eliminate the impostor/s, they win. On the other hand, the impostor/s goal is to eliminate crewmates and fake completion of tasks in order to seamlessly blend in among the crew. If they are able to eliminate crewmates before the completion of tasks until the number of surviving crewmates equals the number of impostors, or if they successfully run down a critical sabotage, they win (Team RDC, 2025). The communication situation to be observed is in the conduct of emergency meetings or dead body reports. When a player reports a dead body or calls for an emergency meeting, all players will deliberate and vote whether to eject a suspected impostor or skip the current voting, serving as the context. In this situation, crewmates can either turn against each other or successfully identify the impostor. Here, the sender is most often than not the player who calls for the meeting or reports the dead body. They will send a message usually stating the location of where they found the body or the reason for calling the meeting. The receivers comprise the rest of the players, who send feedback by questioning suspects, defending their innocence, justifying alibis, or presenting counter-arguments to actively deduce the truth. The channel is the in-game text chat box where the players can send messages. The effect is the final outcome of the meeting, which results in a player being voted out and ejected, a skipped vote, or a tie. The communication situation follows the transactional model, given that the players often work as both senders and receivers with the players sending messages simultaneously in the game chat without taking turns. A potential communication barrier could be psychological, as players experiencing intense emotions like anger or panic can make them overly defensive or offensive, and thus may cause them to deliver or receive arguments poorly. Semantics also present a barrier because the game relies on informal gaming slang (e.g. “sus” meaning suspicious, “vented” referring to the movement of impostors in secret tunnels) that may not be universally understood. Finally, a process barrier may arise from potential technical difficulties or internet connectivity issues given the virtual setting of the game.

PURPOSE


Communication theory provides a standard framework in which communication can be analyzed. As such, the group aims to employ critical analysis to examine communication within the social deception game Among Us. The group seeks to analyze how players communicate with each other under the characteristic deception mechanic of the game. Furthermore, the analysis aims to identify potential communication problems and their impact on the players decision-making throughout the game.

METHODS


There will be a total of four (4) volunteer players in a Google Meet with all group members for the dissemination of instructions. Each player will join a separate Google Meet with 1-2 group members each as observers in order for the player to share their screen for observation throughout the gameplay. The Meet will be recorded for documentation purposes. During emergency meetings or dead body reports, an assigned group member will also screenshot the in-game chatbox for ease of review. The chat logs will be synthesized by breaking down the communication situation into the distinct elements of senders, receivers, messages, feedbacks, channels, context, and communication barriers in order to organize the chaotic exchange into clear components. These will all be presented through the Transactional Model of Communication. The limitation of such methodology is the lack of non-verbal communication cues since the communication will be purely facilitated in a text-only medium. There may also be an observer effect, wherein the players may alter the way they naturally communicate if they know that they are being observed and/or recorded. Additionally, fast-scrolling text can cut off messages or fail to clearly show which messages reply to which, potentially making the context unclear during review.
